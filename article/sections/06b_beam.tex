
\subsection{Thermo-mechanical coupling and viscous dissipation in highly anisotropic plate-beams: an insect-scale wing-like structural benchmark}

The primary technical objectives of this second numerical example are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item To assess the algorithmic robustness of the formulation under large-deformation flexural and torsional kinematics.
    \item To evaluate the performance of the anisotropic constitutive model, using an elongated span-to-width aspect ratio that amplifies the anisotropy-induced bending-torsion coupling and renders it clearly visible.
    \item To quantify the spatial non-uniformity of viscous self-heating induced by the anisotropic, torsional strain state.
    % \item To verify the material stability of the anisotropic model under the combined bending-torsion state by evaluating directional projections of the acoustic tensor.
\end{enumerate}

The anisotropic response is introduced through a transverse-isotropy invariant term added to the isotropic hyperelastic energy, built on the structural tensor of a single fiber direction oriented at \qty{45}{\degree} in-plane. This off-axis alignment is what couples bending and torsion under normal actuation; the invariant formulation itself follows \cite{XX}.

The domain is modeled as a slender cantilever plate-beam fixed at one end and free at the tip (Figure~\ref{fig:beam_geom}). Actuation is driven by activating one electrode layer applying an electric potential across the half-thickness. As in the planar actuator, mass and inertia are omitted from the quasi-static formulation to focus strictly on the constitutive response. While inertial structural dynamics are excluded, a quasi-static actuation with a loading branch and a constant voltage relaxation is prescribed to evaluate both the instantaneous spatial fields and the progressive dissipation build-up over time.

The spatial distribution of key fields at peak electric potential ($t=\qty{0.1}{\second}$) is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:beam_screenshots}. The off-axis fiber orientation induces pronounced coupled twisting alongside the primary flexural motion (Figure~\ref{fig:beam_screenshots}a--b). Crucially, the torsional strain state creates strong spatial non-uniformity in both the First Piola--Kirchhoff stress components (Figure~\ref{fig:beam_screenshots}e--h) and the localized thermoelastic temperature field (Figure~\ref{fig:beam_screenshots}d), demonstrating the capability of the formulation to resolve complex localized dissipation mechanisms in slender structures.

The overall time-dependent response over $t = \qty{0.5}{\second}$ is summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:time_evolution}. The tip pitch and stroke amplitudes track the initial voltage ramp and subsequently stabilize during the hold phase (Figure~\ref{fig:time_evolution}, top). Viscous dissipation and average temperature evolve rapidly during loading before a slow leveling off during relaxation (Figure~\ref{fig:time_evolution}, center and bottom), illustrating the thermo-mechanical relaxation response of the model under quasi-static conditions.
